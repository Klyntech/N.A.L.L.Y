## 1. Competitive Landscape

Lexi operates in the Nigerian legal-tech space. Three tiers of competitors exist:

| Competitor | Type | Transparency | Target |
|---|---|---|---|
| LawPavilion (Primsol) | Incumbent | None (contact sales) | Enterprise / firms |
| ModulawAI | Disruptor | Transparent | Firms / students |
| LawPal AI | Newcomer | Freemium (credits) | Students / solo |

### 1.1 LawPavilion (Primsol)
- Most established player in Nigeria. Dominant for years.
- Multiple products: Prime, LP Elite, CaseManager.
- **No public pricing** — contact sales only.
- Premium, enterprise positioning. Too expensive for students.

### 1.2 ModulawAI
- Transparent, flat, all-inclusive pricing.
- Has a pricing calculator on site. Tiers: Student, Essential, Professional.
- Professional plan = divided by 5 seats for per-seat pricing.
- Replaces 4-5 separate subscriptions (case management, billing, CRM).
- **Key advantage: has LexisNexis access.** This is a serious data moat — LexisNexis is the global gold standard for legal research (case law, statutes, commentary).
- Broad platform, not laser-focused on Nigerian law.

### 1.3 LawPal AI
- Free tier: 100 credits/month (chat, research, drafting).
- Paid tiers: monthly and annual (annual = 1 month free). Exact paid pricing not public — sign up to see it.
- Positioning: "Enterprise features without enterprise price".
- Newcomer, credit-based model.

## 2. The LexisNexis Problem

**ModulawAI having LexisNexis access changes the game.**

- LexisNexis is the gold standard for legal research globally. Massive, verified database of case law, statutes, legal commentary.
- LexisNexis licensing is expensive — usually enterprise pricing.
- If ModulawAI has it, they have a comprehensive, verified data advantage we cannot match on volume alone.

**We cannot out-LEXIS LexisNexis. We must out-user-friendly them.**

LexisNexis is a database, not a chatbot. It is not conversational. It is expensive. Lexi's angle is accessibility, not data volume.

---

## 3. Lexi's Data Strategy (Alternatives to LexisNexis)

1. **OpenLawsNig** — Free, open access to Nigerian laws. Covers statutes and bills. Not as comprehensive as Lexis but solid baseline.
2. **Nigerian Law Reports** — Case law database. Free but less structured.
3. **Build our own** — Scrape and structure legal data from public sources. Takes time but we own it.
4. **Partner with a law firm** — Exchange free Lexi access for their legal library access.
5. **Focus on what LexisNexis can't do** — Real-time conversational chat, document generation, student-friendly UX.

## 4. Positioning Strategy

**The pattern in the market:**
- LawPavilion = incumbent, expensive, enterprise, no transparency.
- ModulawAI = disruptor, transparent, all-in-one, has Lexis data.
- LawPal AI = newcomer, free tier capped at 100 credits, student-focused.

**Where Lexi wins:**
- LawPal's free tier caps at 100 credits. LawPavilion is too expensive for students. ModulawAI is broad (case mgmt, billing, CRM) — not focused on Nigerian law.
- Lexi's play: **Free unlimited Nigerian law chat** + premium tier for document generation. Undercut everyone on price, beat them on focus.

**Core positioning:**
- Make Nigerian law **accessible** — plain-English explanations.
- Target **students and junior lawyers** who can't afford LexisNexis.
- Be the **ChatGPT for Nigerian law** — conversational, not database-style.

**Pricing recommendation:**
- Free tier: unlimited Nigerian law Q&A chat.
- Premium tier: document generation (pleadings, contracts, briefs), citation exports, priority responses.
